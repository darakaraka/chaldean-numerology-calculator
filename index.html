How the calculation works

  • Name number. Add the value of every letter. A total above 9 is the compound number, and repeated digit addition gives the root number from 1 to 9.
  • Compounds over 52. Traditional lists stop at 52, so a larger total has its digits added until it is 52 or less.
  • Birth number. The day of the month. A day from 10 to 31 is the compound number, reduced to a root.
  • Destiny number. Every digit of the full date of birth added together, then reduced.
  • No 9 in the table. The Chaldean system keeps 9 sacred and assigns it to no letter. 9 appears only as a total.
  • No master numbers. 11 and 22 are read as compound numbers with their own meanings and reduce to 2 and 4.
  • Reading sources differ. Compound readings follow the classic tradition for 10 to 32. Sources disagree on 33 to 52, so those show the root reading only.
